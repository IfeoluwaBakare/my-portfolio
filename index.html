RFM Customer Segmentation

Coursework · Databricks (PySpark + SQL)
Complete
DatabricksPySparkSQLAWS S3Lakeview Dashboard

Segmented customers from a FY2020–21 sales dataset (30,000+ transactions, sourced from AWS S3) using Recency, Frequency, and Monetary analysis, to identify which customers are worth the marketing budget and which are at risk of churning.

  • Cleaned the raw transaction data: removed nulls, filtered to valid "complete"/"paid" orders only, excluding cancelled transactions
  • Computed Recency, Frequency, and Monetary metrics per customer using Databricks SQL
  • Scored each customer 1–5 per dimension using WIDTH_BUCKET, capping Frequency and Monetary at the 95th percentile to stop extreme outliers (one customer's spend maxed out at $227K) from skewing the buckets
  • Mapped R×F score combinations into 10 named, actionable segments (Champions, At Risk, Hibernating, etc.) with a tailored marketing action for each
  • Caught and documented a data-integrity edge case: one flagged customer ID had zero valid orders because all 10 of their transactions were cancelled, correctly excluded rather than silently dropped

Coca-Cola vs. PepsiCo: Financial Ratio Analysis

Managerial accounting coursework
Complete
Financial Statement AnalysisLiquidity RatiosProfitability Ratios

Full ratio analysis of Coca-Cola (NYSE: KO) and PepsiCo (NASDAQ: PEP), using each company's balance sheet and income statement to work out which was the stronger, safer investment.

  • Calculated 13 ratios by hand across four categories: short-term liquidity (current, quick, cash), long-term debt (total debt, debt/equity, times interest earned), asset utilization (inventory turnover, receivables turnover, days sales in inventory/receivables), and profitability (gross margin, net margin, return on assets, return on equity)
  • Coca-Cola led on liquidity: current ratio of 1.46 vs. PepsiCo's 0.85, meaning Coca-Cola could cover its short-term obligations far more comfortably
  • Coca-Cola also carried less debt relative to equity (0.69 total debt ratio vs. 0.81) and had a stronger times-interest-earned ratio, signaling lower long-term financial risk
  • PepsiCo came out ahead on operational efficiency: faster inventory turnover (7.37 vs. 4.16) and fewer days sales in inventory (50 vs. 88), meaning it moves product faster
  • Coca-Cola outperformed on every profitability metric measured: gross margin, net margin, return on assets, and return on equity
